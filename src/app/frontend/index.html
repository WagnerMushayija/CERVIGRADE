How it works

Three steps, and a person decides.

01

Register the slide

A technician enters the patient's code and adds the microscope photos. Drag them in or pick them from your files.

02

Read the result

The tool boxes the cells in each photo and grades them. The most serious cell sets the result, and the boxes show where it looked.

03

Confirm or correct

A clinician agrees with each grade or changes it. The overall result follows the clinician's decisions, not the model's.

Who does what

One page, three kinds of account.

There is no sign-up. Every account is created by an administrator, and what you see depends on your role.

Lab technician

UPLOADS

Registers a slide under the patient's code, adds its images, and follows the results. Sees only the slides they uploaded.

Clinician

REVIEWS

Opens the review queue, starts with the most serious slides, and confirms or corrects each grade.

Administrator

MANAGES

The data scientist. Creates and removes accounts, fixes patient codes, deletes records, and sees which model is running.

The grades

Three groups, six exact grades.

Two of the grades look so alike that experts confuse them too, so the page leads with the group and keeps the exact grade underneath.

Negative

NEGATIVE

No sign of abnormal cells.

Low-grade

ASC-US  ·  LSIL

Mild or uncertain changes. These two grades are the ones most often mixed up, by the model and by people.

High-grade

ASC-H  ·  HSIL  ·  SCC

More serious changes, up to cancer. These are the findings a clinician should see first.

Good to know

What this tool can and can't do.

It reads photos, not whole slides.

Upload a microscope photo. A detector finds the cells and each one is graded. It misses about a third of the cells an expert would mark, so a clean result does not prove nothing is there.

It gets most, not all, right.

On slides it had never seen, and with the cells already located by experts, it matched them on about 8 of 10 cells, and on about 9 of 10 when the grades are grouped into three. It is weakest on ASC-US and on the rarest grade, SCC.

It is a prototype.

It has not been tested in a clinic. Images are kept on the computer that runs the server and are labeled only with a patient code, never a name.
